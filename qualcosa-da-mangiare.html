  • Feminine is simple: la (or l' before a vowel) and always le in the plural.
  • Never an apostrophe in the plural: le aranciate, gli espressi.

A portion of…, a glass of…

due porzionithis goes plural

  • Only the first noun changes, as in English: a portion of cake, two portions of cake.
  • un bicchiere di vino rosso → i bicchieri di vino rosso. The wine stays singular, so rosso does too.
  • un bicchiere rosso → i bicchieri rossi. Here the glass is red, so the adjective changes with it.
  • The adjective agrees with the word it describes: la porzione di pasta tonnata calda → le porzioni di pasta tonnata calda (the pasta is hot).
  • Lasagne is already plural in Italian, so cold lasagne is lasagne fredde.

At the bar

  • Il tramezzino: a soft, crustless white-bread triangle with fillings like tomato and mozzarella, egg, or tuna. Sold in bars and eaten as a snack around 11. Never toasted. Your teacher's guess at the word: tra (between) + mezzo (half) + -ino (small). It's usually credited to the poet Gabriele D'Annunzio, as an Italian word for “sandwich”.
  • Lo spuntino: more than a bite, less than a meal. A bit of cheese, a few grapes.
  • Mid-morning, around 10:30: cappuccino e cornetto. A tramezzino comes a bit later.
  • Le lasagne are often eaten cold in summer, packed for a picnic or the beach.
  • L'aperitivo: many bars put out crisps, nuts and small snacks free with your drink.
  • Italy has more juices than the UK: pesca (peach), pera (pear), albicocca (apricot).
  • Order with Vorrei… (I would like…). Vorrei un tè. Freddo o caldo? Bollente, grazie.
